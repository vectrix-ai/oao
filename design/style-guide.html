Full volume.
Zero radius.

OAO's identity is neo-brutalist: a white ground, ink hardware, and three loud flat hues that each hold exactly one job. Everything is drawn with ink borders and hard offset shadows — nothing blurs, nothing rounds, nothing fades. It runs at two volumes: full volume on marketing surfaces, and a quieter product tier in the console, where the same hardware thins out so the tool stays comfortable all day. The landing page (vectrix-ai/oao-landing-page) is the reference implementation of the poster tier; this guide defines both.

The logo

The mark is hardware too.

The approved exports live in style-guide-assets/ (mirrored in the landing repo's public/brand/). Use them as-is: never recreate the wordmark with a font, never stretch it, never recolor it. Black on light grounds, white on ink grounds.

Color

Three hues, three jobs.

The ground is white, the hardware is ink. On top of that, exactly three flat brand hues — and each one has a fixed job. Never blend them, never tint them, never run a gradient through them. If a hue is doing a job that isn't listed here, it's the wrong hue.

Ground --canvas / --surface #ffffff White page, white cards. --surface-2 #f5f5f2 for quiet wells (sidebars, message grounds).
Ink --line / --ink-950 #111111 All borders, all shadows, all text. Also the ground of dark bands and "dead" states (crashes, terminated).
Action red --primary #fa3d31 Primary buttons only. In the product it may extend to destructive confirmation and error text — nothing else.
Signal yellow --highlight #f7ff00 Eyebrows, highlights, tickers, live/active states, status stamps, selected chips. The accent color on ink grounds.
Accent blue --accent #3d5bf5 Links, italic headline accents, sequence markers (01/02/03), focus rings. Light grounds only — it drowns on ink.

No theme toggle. The old green system had a dark theme; the new system doesn't. There is one light system, and dark is used compositionally — full-bleed ink bands and ink cards inside a light page (see "Dark grounds" below). Do not build a data-theme="dark" variant of the console.

Typography

Three clear voices.

Space Grotesk speaks for the interface, Inter for paragraphs, Space Mono for the machine. This replaces the old stack (Manrope / Inter / IBM Plex Mono) everywhere.

Shape & elevation

One hardware, two volumes.

Zero border radius everywhere — the --radius-* tokens are 0 and stay 0. The same brutalist hardware runs at two volumes. Poster tier (marketing pages, empty states, onboarding set-pieces): 2–3px borders, shadow-m/l, big presses. Product tier — the console default: 1px hairlines for rows and internal chrome, 1.5px ink borders for controls and cards, shadow-s (2px) at most on resting elements, and --line-soft (ink-200) for dividers inside panels. Shadows are still hard offsets — never a blur — but in the product they whisper: shadow-m/l is reserved for things that genuinely float (modals, popovers, toasts).

1px soft — dividers
1px — rows, tags
1.5px + shadow-s — controls
2px + shadow-m — overlays
3px + shadow-l — poster

Motion follows the shadow — gently. In the product tier, buttons press into the page by 1px on hover and 2px on active (shadow shrinks, then disappears); cards lift by 1px. The 2–4px presses belong to the poster tier. Entrances rise ~18px with a fast ease-out. Story animations (status flips, event rows landing) play once, then rest in their final state. Always honor prefers-reduced-motion by showing the finished state, static.

Status stamp semantics: yellow = live or successfully finished (RUNNING, RESUMED, DONE ✓); white + ink text = at rest (PAUSED); white + gray text = waiting (QUEUED); ink block + white text = dead (FAILED, crashed workers). Stamps are Space Mono 700, ~10.5px, letter-spacing 0.1em, 1.5px border, no shadow.

Dark grounds

Ink is a place, not a theme.

Dark surfaces are deliberate set-pieces on the light page: a full-bleed --ink-950 band with a 3px border and shadow-l, or an ink card inside a light grid. On ink: text is #f5f5f1, muted copy is --ink-300, hairlines are --ink-700, small offset shadows may use #f5f5f1, and the accent switches from blue to signal yellow.

Boring where it matters.

Muted copy on ink uses --ink-300. Accent italics go yellow — blue drowns here.

White panel on ink — must reset color to ink text explicitly, or it inherits the band's light text and becomes unreadable. A 3px yellow border lifts it off the band.
The console

The session view is the poster.

The design target for the console's session view is the ConsoleSession mock in the landing repo (src/components/ConsoleSession.astro) — a working specimen of every pattern above. Its anatomy, top to bottom:

  • 01Chrome bar — ink strip, Space Mono URL/crumb, small square window dots (one yellow).
  • 02Header — mono breadcrumb in --ink-500, Space Grotesk title, mono meta chips (1px borders: agent · version, attempt, duration, tokens, cost), status stamp on the right.
  • 03Timing ruler — segmented bar behind a 1.5px border: blue segments for model time, white for tool time, ink for the crash gap, yellow after resume. Tick labels in 9.5px mono.
  • 04Transcript — user message on --surface-2; collapsible reasoning/tool rows (1px border, ▸, duration right-aligned); crash rows as ink blocks; resume rows on yellow; the agent's answer in a 1.5px card with shadow-s.
  • 05Session panel — --surface-2 sidebar behind a 1.5px divider: ID, status, agent, model preset, tools, usage, cost as label/value pairs with --line-soft hairlines. Hidden under ~940px.
  • 06Composer — 1.5px bordered field with shadow-s and a red SEND button.
Ground rules

Do / don't.

  • DOUse semantic tokens (--primary, --accent, --highlight, --line, --muted) rather than raw hex values.
  • DOKeep machine data (IDs, presets, fences, tokens, costs) in Space Mono, honestly labeled — "est." when estimated.
  • DOReset text color to ink on any white panel placed on an ink ground.
  • DOKeep the console at product volume: 1–1.5px borders, shadow-s or none at rest, 1px presses. Save shadow-m/l and 2–3px borders for overlays and set-pieces.
  • DON'TRound a corner, blur a shadow, or run a gradient. Anywhere.
  • DON'TTint the brand hues (no #fdffd8-style washes) or invent new accent colors.
  • DON'TUse action red for anything but primary buttons, destructive confirmation, and error text.
  • DON'TPut accent blue on ink grounds — switch to signal yellow.
  • DON'TRecreate, stretch, or recolor the logo, or set the wordmark in a font.
