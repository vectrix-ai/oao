# OAO Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We commit to making OAO's community welcoming, safe, and equitable. Everyone
who participates in good faith and follows this code receives the same
opportunity to contribute. We respect the dignity, rights, perspectives, and
work of people of every background and identity.

## Expected behavior

Community members should:

1. Keep discussions relevant to OAO and its community.
2. Communicate with kindness, honesty, and professional care.
3. Respect different viewpoints, experiences, cultures, and communication
   styles.
4. Give and receive constructive feedback gracefully.
5. Take responsibility for their words, actions, and contributions.
6. Acknowledge impact, make amends where possible, and help repair harm.
7. Protect confidential and private information.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior, including threats or encouragement of it, is not
acceptable:

- harassment, intimidation, stalking, or continued unwanted attention;
- insults, character attacks, trolling, or demeaning comments;
- stereotyping, discrimination, or exclusion based on identity or personal
  characteristics;
- sexualized language, imagery, or attention inappropriate to the community;
- publishing or using another person's private information without permission;
- threats of violence or conduct that endangers a person or group;
- impersonation or identity deception intended to evade moderation;
- presenting another person's work without appropriate credit;
- unsolicited promotion or commercial material outside community norms;
- irresponsible sharing of material that depicts, facilitates, or amplifies
  prohibited conduct; or
- other conduct that reasonably threatens the safety or well-being of the OAO
  community.

## Scope

This code applies to OAO repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, and other project spaces. It also applies when someone officially
represents OAO, such as through an official account or as an appointed
representative at an online or in-person event.

## Reporting

Do not open a public issue to report a conduct incident.

For conduct connected to content on GitHub, use the content's menu and choose
**Report content**. When GitHub offers the choice, select **Report to repository
admins** so OAO's repository administrators can review it. If that option is
not available, continue with GitHub's reporting flow so GitHub can review the
content. Reports about a repository administrator should be sent to GitHub
rather than to that administrator.

Include the relevant links and enough context for the report to be assessed,
but do not expose credentials, customer or personal data, private prompts or
transcripts, or vulnerability details in public content.

## Review and confidentiality

Repository administrators responsible for moderation will review reports in a
timely, impartial manner. Anyone with a conflict of interest will recuse
themselves. Moderators may review the reported content and related project
records, request more context through GitHub, and speak with involved people.

Reports, identities, and enforcement details will be shared only as needed to
investigate, protect participants, apply an outcome, or meet legal obligations.
Moderators will favor private communication and will not tolerate retaliation
against reporters, witnesses, or people who help resolve an incident.

## Enforcement and repair

Moderators will consider the conduct's impact, severity, context, history, and
the safety of the community. Depending on those factors, they may skip steps in
this progression:

1. **Warning:** a private written explanation of the violation and expected
   correction, which may include acknowledging impact or making an apology.
2. **Limited participation:** a defined cooling-off period or restrictions on
   particular interactions, topics, or GitHub features.
3. **Temporary suspension:** time-limited removal from OAO project spaces, with
   clear conditions for return where return is appropriate.
4. **Permanent ban:** removal from OAO community spaces when repeated or severe
   conduct makes continued participation unsafe.

Moderators may also hide, edit, or remove content; lock conversations; reject
contributions; revoke repository access; or take other proportionate action
available through GitHub. Where safe and practical, outcomes will identify a
path to repair. A permanent ban is reserved for cases where lesser measures
cannot adequately protect the community.

This progression guides consistent decisions but does not prevent moderators
from using judgment to protect participants. Enforcement discussions occur
privately with the people involved; broader communication may occur when it is
necessary for safety or is part of an agreed resolution.
